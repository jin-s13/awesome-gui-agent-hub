benchmark

GUI/web/mobile/OS automation datasets, environments, tasks, metrics, and benchmarks

62 Methodology quality 99 Recency 38 Reproducibility 48 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Realistic GUI/OS benchmark and interaction task design

Creates environment-faithful task distributions for desktop, dynamic short-video, drag-based, and video-guided computer-use settings so capabilities can be compared beyond narrow click/type benchmarks.

Open edge: Coverage is still environment- or action-specific; cross-OS, cross-app, cross-interface, and longer-horizon real-world generalization is not established.
Agent capability improvement via skills, supervision, and task synthesis

Addresses grounding and policy learning gaps through multimodal skill artifacts, weakly supervised screenshots, verifiable RL task generation, and task-aware keyframe selection.

Open edge: Evidence is concentrated on selected benchmarks; transfer to unseen applications, efficiency under different data/compute budgets, and robustness over dynamic interfaces remain unreported or unverified.
Fine-grained metrics, diagnostics, and uncertainty stress-testing

Moves beyond aggregate success to sub-goal completion, cost-accuracy information efficiency, and post-hoc uncertainty/conformal safety for GUI grounding.

Open edge: Proxy metrics are not yet aligned with user decision utility; uncertainty ranking transfer across model tiers and interfaces is weak; multi-step trajectory uncertainty is not covered.
Cross-model baselines and reproducibility artifacts

Makes results auditable by comparing multiple frontier frameworks/models and, in some cases, releasing deterministic rule-based scoring or analysis artifacts.

Open edge: Several papers lack visible limitations or complete code/dataset/repository signals, so stability across implementations, compute budgets, and benchmark versions remains uncertain.

Shared Direction

  • Realistic task distributions expose gaps that coarse click/type evaluations miss.
  • Fine-grained metrics such as sub-goal completion, cost-accuracy, and uncertainty add diagnostic value beyond aggregate success rates.
  • Frontier VLM agents still struggle with dynamic observation, multi-step execution, and interaction types beyond simple clicks.
  • Scalable task synthesis, weak supervision, and multimodal skill retrieval are promising directions for reducing annotation or improving grounding.
  • Benchmarking requires controlled environments and deterministic or reproducible scoring where possible.

Key Differences

  • Interaction space: drag-only, GUI+CLI desktop, dynamic short-video, and video-guided tasks emphasize different capability boundaries and are not directly comparable.
  • Evaluation target: single-step GUI grounding, multi-step task completion, and efficiency-aware task completion use different success definitions.
  • Supervision and adaptation: weak supervision from unannotated screenshots, online RL with verifiable synthesized tasks, multimodal skill libraries, and keyframe extraction rely on different data and compute assumptions.
  • Metric priority: Pass@1, sub-goal completion, cost-accuracy, and conformal uncertainty reflect different views of reliability and usefulness.
  • Reproducibility posture: some works provide rule-based scoring or public uncertainty records while others have missing limitation or artifact signals, making cross-paper auditability uneven.

Open Questions

  • Do fine-grained benchmark metrics transfer across desktop, web, mobile, and dynamic short-video environments without target-specific recalibration?
  • Why do uncertainty ranking methods transfer well within a model family but poorly across model tiers and observable interfaces?
  • How should agents learn or regulate observation in dynamic interfaces to avoid over- and under-observation?
  • Do skill retrieval, weak supervision, and verifiable RL gains persist on unseen applications or operating systems beyond the reported benchmarks?
  • Which minimum dataset, code, metric, baseline, and limitation artifacts are needed to verify benchmark conclusions when several packet entries lack those signals?

method

GUI agent models, planning, grounding, action prediction, training, or reasoning methods

61 Methodology quality 99 Recency 39 Reproducibility 47 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

GUI grounding and action-space benchmarks

Defines evaluation surfaces for action prediction and grounding across click, drag, GUI-plus-CLI, video-guided, and dynamic short-video interfaces.

Open edge: Coverage is often single-platform or single-step; transfer to multi-step trajectories, unseen interfaces, and different model classes remains unevidenced.
Training-data synthesis and weak supervision for GUI policies

Reduces human annotation burden through verifiable synthetic task generation, unannotated screenshot learning, or interaction-specific supervised datasets.

Open edge: Attribution among task synthesis, RL, data scale, and model capacity is unclear; several works lack explicit limitations or full reproducibility details.
Inference-time context and observation control

Improves long-horizon and dynamic interfaces by compacting context, selecting task-relevant keyframes, or jointly scoring accuracy and information efficiency.

Open edge: Whether these mechanisms correct over-observation/under-observation deficits and generalize beyond benchmark-specific environments is not established.
Uncertainty and fine-grained evaluation for grounding

Measures sub-goal differences hidden by similar success rates and calibrates uncertainty or conformal click regions for reliability.

Open edge: Findings are mostly limited to single-step grounding; cross-model/cross-interface UQ transfer is weak and coverage degrades under distribution or interface shift.

Shared Direction

  • Current GUI agents still fall short of human-level reliability on dynamic or long-horizon tasks; aggregate success alone hides meaningful failure structure.
  • Grounding and action prediction are increasingly evaluated through task-specific environment metrics such as sub-goal completion, cost-accuracy, and calibrated spatial coverage.
  • Several works pursue sample or annotation efficiency, whether via synthetic verifiable tasks, weak supervision from screenshots, keyframe extraction, context compaction, or interaction-specific datasets.
  • The evidence base has verification gaps: many top papers report strong results but provide no limitation field or limited artifact/reproducibility signals.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation target differs: some works optimize task success and sub-goal completion; others optimize cost-accuracy under limited observation; others evaluate uncertainty ranking and conformal click safety.
  • Interaction setting differs: static macOS desktop with mixed GUI and CLI, long-horizon mobile, dynamic short-video, video-guided, and drag-specific action spaces create different boundary conditions.
  • Method of improvement differs: online RL with verifiable task generation, two-stage weak supervision from unannotated data, supervised fine-tuning on drag/interaction data, and inference-time context/keyframe management.
  • Evaluation assumptions differ: deterministic rule-based scoring with task ground truth versus post-hoc uncertainty calibration with ranked UQ methods imply different generalization and deployment contracts.

Open Questions

  • Do reported gains on OSWorld, ScienceBoard, MemGUI-Bench, MacAgentBench, and short-video settings transfer to unseen operating systems, interfaces, or closed-source model tiers?
  • How much of the measured advantage on macOS and online RL tasks is attributable to skill libraries, task priors, framework design, or synthetic data rather than the proposed method?
  • Can over-observation and under-observation in dynamic interfaces be corrected by proactive context management or keyframe selection, or do they require new observation-control learning objectives?
  • Can single-step uncertainty and conformal grounding results extend to multi-step agent trajectories and models with inaccessible internal states?

theory

Formal analysis, task abstractions, interaction theory, or reliability/safety framing for GUI agents

62 Methodology quality 99 Recency 39 Reproducibility 49 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Formalizing interaction regimes and benchmark boundaries

Produces task abstractions, scoring rules, and failure-mode evidence for desktop, dynamic short-video, and drag interactions, including fine-grained multi-checkpoint and cost-accuracy signals.

Open edge: Coverage is bounded to selected platforms and interaction types; formal assumptions are not directly matched to model behavior beyond observed failure modes, and several artifacts are not visible.
Scaling and training-data efficiency

Reduces reliance on costly annotation or dense human rewards through verifiable task synthesis, online RL, weak supervision from unannotated screenshots, and visual context segmentation.

Open edge: Limitations are largely absent in this evidence, and direct validation that these methods predict failures or transfer beyond reported benchmarks is missing.
Skill and context management for long-horizon control

Improves long-horizon and UI-grounded workflows by retrieving multimodal skill artifacts or by emitting context-management actions that keep compact but informative state.

Open edge: The reported gains depend on specific base models and frameworks; out-of-distribution generalization is only partially tested, and robustness across application/interface changes remains unclear.
Reliability quantification for GUI grounding

Benchmarks post-hoc uncertainty quantification methods and conformal click regions, separating stable open-weight UQ families from weak cross-tier transfer.

Open edge: Evaluation is limited to single-step executable grounding rather than multi-step agent trajectories, and coverage degrades under calibration/test or interface mismatch.

Shared Direction

  • The field treats GUI agents as vision-language policies over screen or UI states and evaluates them with task success plus finer metrics rather than pass rate alone.
  • There is broad agreement that click-only evaluation under-represents real computer use, motivating coverage of mixed GUI/CLI, dynamic content, drag interactions, and long-horizon context.
  • Method papers justify gains through baseline comparisons on benchmark suites and often attribute improvements to curated data, skill libraries, or context mechanisms rather than architecture alone.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation targets differ: some measure general desktop task completion, while others target dynamic content cost-accuracy, drag grounding, uncertainty ranking, or long-horizon mobile control.
  • Supervision and training regimes differ: verifiable RL synthesis, weak supervision from unannotated screenshots, retrieved multimodal skills, and supervised context actions each make different data assumptions.
  • Deployment settings differ substantially, including macOS desktop, browser-based short-video environments, mobile devices, and single-step grounding, implying different observability and action spaces.
  • Capability-focused work prioritizes success-rate improvements, while reliability-focused work prioritizes selective uncertainty ranking and conformal spatial coverage, revealing a tension between state-of-the-art claims and safety-oriented evaluation.

Open Questions

  • How do different task formalizations and metric choices change model rankings or training targets across platforms, interaction types, and model tiers?
  • Do verifiable task synthesis and weak supervision predict failure and transfer to environments beyond the reported benchmarks?
  • Can uncertainty-quantification rankings transfer across model classes and observable interfaces without target-specific reranking?
  • Which context-management or multimodal skill representations preserve long-horizon reliability when the base vision-language model or agent framework changes?

system

Runnable GUI agent systems, frameworks, sandboxes, tool-use platforms, or orchestration layers

59 Methodology quality 99 Recency 39 Reproducibility 46 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

End-to-end GUI/computer-use agent training and orchestration frameworks

Turns policies into runnable agents that execute multi-step computer tasks across websites, desktops, and harnesses; reduces annotated data needs through weak supervision, online RL, or verifiable rewards.

Open edge: Error recovery remains weak after training; harness design affects trainability; cross-environment deployment and independent reproduction outside reported benchmarks are not yet demonstrated.
Multimodal skill and memory augmentation for GUI agents

Improves UI grounding and workflow verification by storing structured visual/text skill artifacts or action-grounded image crops rather than relying only on full screenshots.

Open edge: Generalization across applications and operating systems; differences between offline evaluation and live interaction; independent comparison of memory representations is missing.
Diagnostic evaluation of GUI transitions and failure modes

Measures whether models understand action-induced state changes, source tracking, and task-relevant GUI transitions instead of evaluating only end-task success.

Open edge: Benchmarks are offline and limited to Linux or predefined action types; step-level diagnostics do not yet establish predictive validity for asynchronous, real-time GUI interaction.
Safety and red-teaming infrastructure for computer-using agents

Audits prompt injection and skill injection resistance across browser and coding surfaces, and checks whether previously published attack results are reproducible.

Open edge: Only hand-crafted attack templates were tested; RL-optimized injection strings were not reproduced; safety hardening appears domain-conditioned and does not generalize across surfaces.

Shared Direction

  • Current systems emphasize multimodal visual grounding, using screenshots and image-derived representations rather than relying only on DOM or accessibility metadata.
  • Training and evaluation are shifting toward executable agent loops: policy rollouts, trajectory-level rewards, and live environment interaction instead of static single-step prediction.
  • Memory, context management, and skill/tool retrieval are recognized as core components for multi-step GUI task performance.
  • Open-source frameworks aim to lower data and infrastructure cost while matching larger proprietary agents, but verification remains mostly benchmark-limited.

Key Differences

  • Interaction representation differs: some systems act via native screenshots and keyboard/mouse only, while others wrap harnesses or call tools, affecting controllability and trainability.
  • Supervision and data scale differ: weak supervision from unannotated screenshots and tiny annotation budgets versus cloud-scale verifiable rollouts and online RL.
  • Evaluation emphasis differs: end-task success metrics, step-level transition understanding, and adversarial attack success rates are not directly comparable.
  • Memory design differs: full-image visual memory, action-grounded local crops, and hierarchical multimodal skill libraries make different assumptions about which visual context should be retained.

Open Questions

  • Can RL-trained GUI agents substantially improve error recovery, given evidence that recovery remains weak after training?
  • Does safety hardening transfer across application domains, especially from browser tasks to coding or tool-use tasks?
  • Do offline step-level diagnostics of GUI transition understanding predict live end-task reliability under asynchronous timing and delays?
  • How do these systems generalize across operating systems, action vocabularies, and independently reproduced infrastructure settings?

application

Domain applications of GUI agents, such as office automation, mobile apps, web tasks, software operation, or professional tools

64 Methodology quality 99 Recency 37 Reproducibility 50 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Real-world desktop and cross-platform computer-use benchmarking

Measures GUI agent competence on realistic desktop applications and cross-platform web, mobile, and desktop tasks with fine-grained outcomes rather than only final success.

Open edge: Evidence is mostly narrow per platform or per task; whether fine-grained desktop metrics transfer across operating systems, applications, and real professional workflows remains unverified.
Multimodal skill libraries and reusable task programs

Improves computer-use agents by retrieving prior application knowledge or compiling successful runs into fast replays, reducing repeated reasoning cost and grounding actions in visual artifacts.

Open edge: Visual skill benefits and replay speedups are shown on selected benchmarks; stale or mismatched screens, unseen applications, and whether reusable artifacts cover dynamic UI changes remain open.
Dynamic, video-guided, and observation-sensitive GUI tasks

Evaluates and improves agents when interface content changes over time or when instruction is provided through video and keyframes, including observation cost and task accuracy trade-offs.

Open edge: Current agents lack observation control and often over-observe or under-observe; keyframe extraction gains are mostly reported on video QA, not yet on broad GUI agentic task transfer.
Specialized interaction grounding and execution correction

Covers less click-centric interactions such as drag manipulations and intercepts execution errors via history-aware, visually grounded critics before actions are taken.

Open edge: Drag and critic results are demonstrated on limited suites; combining specialized interaction grounding with cross-platform critic generalization and real-world failure costs is not shown.

Shared Direction

  • Visual grounding matters across tasks: screenshots, UI elements, or visual skill artifacts are central to GUI agent performance and failure diagnosis.
  • Refined evaluation beyond final success is needed, including sub-goal completion, cost-accuracy, and benign recall under controlled conditions.
  • Real or faithfully simulated application environments are necessary because text-only or static benchmarks miss execution errors, observation behavior, and interaction coverage.
  • Current agents show a shared failure pattern: reasonable planning but unreliable grounding, observation control, or repeated-task efficiency.

Key Differences

  • Works target different domains and interaction modes, from static desktop or web tasks to dynamic feeds, drag interaction, video-guided tasks, and security-sensitive navigation, implying different observation and action models.
  • Intervention choices differ: one line supplies external skill retrieval, another compiles replays, another adds a critic, and another selects task-relevant keyframes, addressing different bottlenecks.
  • Evaluation targets differ—aggregate task accuracy, Pass@1, cost-accuracy, sub-goal completion, or benign recall—making direct comparison difficult.
  • Some works provide benchmarks without a new intervention, while others propose method-level changes; the evidence therefore disagrees on whether gains come from the model, framework, or external memory or skill components.

Open Questions

  • Can skill retrieval, replay, critic, and keyframe gains transfer across operating systems, applications, and dynamically changing interfaces?
  • How should agents balance observation cost and task accuracy in streaming or video-based interfaces where both over-observation and under-observation degrade results?
  • Which evaluation signal beyond Pass@1—sub-goal completion, cost-accuracy, benign recall—is most predictive of real desktop, mobile, and professional workflow reliability?
  • How robust are current specialized benchmarks to unseen apps, languages, adversarial content, and real-world cues such as URLs and reputation metadata?
  • Do current application benchmarks and methods provide sufficient code, data, baseline, and limitation evidence to support reproduction and generalization claims?

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Taxonomy and roadmap synthesis

Organizes fragmented GUI-agent work into candidate directions: training and scaling, evaluation benchmarks, safety, uncertainty, long-horizon interaction, and tutorial generation.

Open edge: The packet does not include formal survey papers or systematic inclusion criteria, so taxonomy coverage and representativeness cannot be verified.
Comparative evaluation synthesis

Summarizes how evaluation choices such as masked reputation cues, domain-specific tasks, full trajectories, and final-screenshot reward shape conclusions across GUI agent studies.

Open edge: Results are not clearly compatible across benchmarks because tasks, interfaces, metrics, baselines, and reward conditions differ, while several limitation details are missing.
Open problem and gap mapping

Turns broad evidence into concrete gaps: weak UQ transfer across model tiers, low benign recall under sanitized fraud cues, domain-conditioned safety failures, evaluator reward noise, and long-horizon context management.

Open edge: Gap prioritization is not established; evidence is concentrated in 2024-2026 and often limited to single-step, browser, mobile, or video-specific settings.

Shared Direction

  • Benchmark-driven evaluation is the dominant evidence model; success rate, ranking transfer, or attack success are used to judge GUI/computer-use agents.
  • VLM-based agents and evaluators are central, but their reliability degrades under domain, interface, or distribution shift.
  • Training-oriented work increasingly relies on verifiable or synthetic tasks and autonomous reward signals rather than only manually labeled demonstrations.
  • Robust generalization remains difficult: findings from one benchmark, model tier, or domain often do not transfer to another.
  • The packet separates popularity and read-first signals from methodology and reproducibility signals; several high-scoring papers lack limitation details or artifact evidence, which is a verification gap.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation object differs: single-step executable grounding, multi-step browser or mobile tasks, fraud judgment, or video-guided GUI control.
  • Supervision and training differ: verifiable task synthesis with online RL, supervised context management, RL with noise-corrected evaluator feedback, or no training at all.
  • Interaction modes differ: static screenshot grounding, long-horizon mobile UI, browser-domain safety, video keyframes, and multimodal tutorial generation.
  • Safety and uncertainty conclusions conflict: UQ ranking transfer is strong within model but weak across model tiers; browser prompt-injection resistance contrasts with coding-domain skill-injection vulnerability.
  • Benchmark protocols impose different information constraints: sanitized URL and reputation cues for fraud, masked protocols for safety audits, and final-screenshot reward for RL.

Open Questions

  • Which gap should be prioritized first for deployment: uncertainty transfer, long-horizon context, domain-conditioned safety, or reward noise?
  • Can UQ ranking and safety-resistance findings transfer across model tiers and interfaces without target-specific re-evaluation?
  • How comparable are success rates across OSWorld, ScienceBoard, MemGUI-Bench, MobileWorld, and video-guided benchmarks when tasks and metrics differ?
  • Do conclusions based on single-step grounding or browser-only safety generalize to multi-step, cross-domain, and real-world GUI workflows?
  • What systematic inclusion and quality criteria should a formal GUI-agent survey use, given that the available evidence packet contains primary studies rather than full survey texts?
