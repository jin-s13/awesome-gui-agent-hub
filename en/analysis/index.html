benchmark

GUI/web/mobile/OS automation datasets, environments, tasks, metrics, and benchmarks

61 Methodology quality 99 Recency 38 Reproducibility 46 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Realistic GUI/computer-use task suites and datasets (e.g., MacAgentBench, LivingScreen, DragOn, VG-GUIBench)

Creates shared tasks and environments that make agent capabilities comparable across desktop, browser, dynamic content, video-guided, and drag-based interactions.

Open edge: Whether these task distributions cover long-horizon multi-application workflows, unseen OS/app configurations, and dynamic-interface variants; the provided evidence lacks full limitation or artifact detail for some suites.
Fine-grained metrics and stress-test design (e.g., multi-checkpoint scoring, cost-accuracy, uncertainty quantification, drag grounding evaluation)

Probes agent behavior beyond aggregate success: sub-goal completion, observation efficiency, selective uncertainty ranking transfer, conformal click coverage, and interaction-type-specific grounding.

Open edge: Alignment between these diagnostic metrics and downstream deployment reliability is not established; uncertainty ranking transfers weakly across model classes and interfaces, and conformal coverage degrades under distribution shift.
Benchmark-driven baselines, training, and reporting (e.g., VISUALSKILL, ScaleCUA, GUICrafter using OSWorld, ScienceBoard, CUA-World, OSExpert-Eval)

Makes progress comparable by using shared benchmark scores, rule-based or verifiable rewards, and common baselines for skill libraries, online RL, and weak supervision.

Open edge: Benchmark conclusion stability across implementations, frameworks, and compute budgets is mostly untested; several systems report strong scores but the provided evidence does not show full artifact or reporting details.

Shared Direction

  • Aggregate task success alone is insufficient; fine-grained sub-goal, efficiency, or diagnostic metrics reveal meaningful model differences.
  • Realistic, application-grounded environments are favored over synthetic static settings, with rule-based or human references used for scoring.
  • Frontier visual-language agents still fall below reliable human-level performance on dynamic or long-horizon computer-use tasks, especially in observation control and uncertainty transfer.

Key Differences

  • Interaction scope: some benchmark static desktop or drag actions, while others target dynamic living-screen content or video-guided sequences.
  • Supervision and data: some use verifiable rule-based task synthesis, others use massive unannotated screenshots, and others use multimodal skill artifacts.
  • Evaluation target: some focus on task success and sub-goal progress, others on observation cost-accuracy, single-step grounding uncertainty, or drag completion.

Open Questions

  • Do current GUI/computer-use benchmark tasks transfer to unseen OS/app configurations and long-horizon multi-application workflows?
  • Which fine-grained metrics best predict deployment reliability when uncertainty ranking transfer is weak across model classes and interfaces?
  • Can verifiable task synthesis and weak-supervision pipelines produce stable benchmark conclusions across frameworks and compute budgets without overfitting benchmark-specific reward signals?

method

GUI agent models, planning, grounding, action prediction, training, or reasoning methods

60 Methodology quality 99 Recency 38 Reproducibility 45 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Screen grounding and interaction-space benchmarks

Expands actionable GUI evaluation beyond static click tasks to drag, dynamic short-video, macOS GUI+CLI, and video-guided interactions.

Open edge: Generalization outside the reported platform or interaction type; observation control for dynamic content remains unsolved.
Training and data-scaling methods

Reduces annotation dependence via weak supervision from unannotated screenshots, or scales online RL with verifiable synthesized tasks.

Open edge: Whether reported gains are attributable to the learning algorithm, task-synthesis pipeline, sampling strategy, or baseline implementation choices is not isolated.
Long-horizon context and planning methods

Maintains useful context over long mobile GUI trajectories by treating context management as first-class policy actions and using compact structured context fields.

Open edge: Cross-benchmark evidence is narrow; it is unclear whether context-management actions transfer beyond the trained benchmark family.
Uncertainty and evaluation-protocol methods

Separates model ranking from fine-grained step completion and quantifies grounding confidence via post-hoc uncertainty methods for safer action prediction.

Open edge: Current uncertainty benchmarks stop at single-step grounding; multi-step trajectory risk and weak cross-model/cross-interface transfer are not covered.

Shared Direction

  • GUI agents are consistently framed as vision-language action grounding: the model observes a screen or video context and outputs executable interface actions.
  • Evaluation is moving toward richer interaction coverage and finer scoring, including sub-goal completion, cost-accuracy, drag interactions, dynamic content, and multi-app GUI+CLI tasks.
  • Training innovations aim to lower human annotation cost or increase task coverage through weak supervision, curriculum learning, and verifiable online RL.
  • Reported improvements usually depend on multiple components, so separation of model, policy, data, and evaluation effects remains an evidence gap.

Key Differences

  • Single-step executable grounding is treated as a proxy for safe computer use in uncertainty work, while other work evaluates long-horizon task success, leading to different definitions of agent capability.
  • Several benchmarks assume static screens or click/drag interfaces, whereas others target dynamic living-screen content that changes between actions.
  • Supervision strategy varies: weakly supervised visual grounding from unlabeled screenshots, SFT on annotated trajectories, and online RL with synthesized verifiable tasks.
  • Context handling varies: passive history accumulation is replaced by proactive context management in some agents, while dynamic-interface work focuses on observation control rather than memory policy.
  • Uncertainty transfer is reported stable within a model family but weak across open/closed model tiers, so calibration requirements differ by deployment setting.

Open Questions

  • What factors make an agent trained or evaluated on one interaction type transfer to drag, dynamic, multi-app, or out-of-distribution mobile settings?
  • How much of the gain in scaled computer-use or desktop benchmarks comes from the learned policy versus surrounding skill libraries, task synthesis, or sampling strategies?
  • Can observation-control behavior be learned and evaluated beyond short-video platforms to reduce over- and under-observation?
  • How should uncertainty quantification extend from single-step grounding to long-horizon GUI trajectories and closed-source deployments where internal states are unavailable?
  • What minimum ablation and reproducibility evidence is needed to isolate method contribution when limitation fields are missing?

theory

Formal analysis, task abstractions, interaction theory, or reliability/safety framing for GUI agents

61 Methodology quality 99 Recency 38 Reproducibility 46 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Fine-grained and dynamic task evaluation

Defines GUI-agent task mixes beyond click-only success, including desktop GUI+CLI tasks, drag interactions, dynamic short-video interfaces, and multi-checkpoint or cost-accuracy scoring.

Open edge: Whether these evaluation abstractions predict real-world task success and transfer outside the represented platforms, especially for dynamic content and observation control, remains unclear.
Scalable supervision and online reinforcement learning

Reduces dependence on costly human annotations through verifiable task synthesis, weak supervision from unannotated screenshots, and sample-efficient rollout allocation for computer-use training.

Open edge: Direct evidence on generalization beyond OSWorld/ScienceBoard or small annotated calibration sets is limited; limitation and reproducibility details are often unreported.
Long-horizon skill and context management

Improves multi-step agent behavior by giving agents proactive context actions or multimodal skill retrieval instead of passive history accumulation.

Open edge: Whether gains persist across frameworks, applications, and deployment domains beyond the evaluated mobile or desktop suites remains unresolved.
Reliability and uncertainty framing

Evaluates post-hoc uncertainty and spatial calibration for GUI grounding so confidence quality can be assessed separately from task success.

Open edge: Single-step grounding evidence does not cover multi-step trajectories; cross-model and cross-interface uncertainty transfer is weak under distribution shift.

Shared Direction

  • Recent work converges on moving beyond end-to-end success rates by measuring sub-goal completion, interaction coverage, cost or efficiency, and calibration quality.
  • Current GUI agents show observable capability gaps in observation control, long-horizon context handling, and fine-grained interaction types.
  • Synthetic, weakly supervised, or retrieved external knowledge is treated as a key lever for improving agents without relying only on manual annotation.
  • Several papers expose verification or reproducibility gaps through empty limitation fields, sparse artifact signals, or weak transfer beyond the evaluated setting.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation target: success-oriented outcomes versus fine-grained sub-goal and multi-checkpoint metrics versus uncertainty or calibration quality versus cost-accuracy trade-offs.
  • Interaction span: single-step executable grounding, multi-step long-horizon trajectories, and pre-action skill retrieval make different assumptions about what an agent must control.
  • Supervision and training route: verifiable synthetic online RL tasks, weak supervision from unannotated screenshots, supervised fine-tuning with context-management annotations, and frozen external skill libraries.
  • Deployment scope: static desktop and mobile settings, dynamic short-video interfaces, drag-specific interaction coverage, and GUI+CLI mixed environments create different assumptions about observation and action space.

Open Questions

  • Which fine-grained evaluation signals best predict deployment success and transfer across unseen interfaces?
  • How can observation control be designed and measured beyond a single dynamic platform?
  • Under what conditions do uncertainty ranking and calibration transfer from open-weight single-step grounding to closed-source multi-step agents?
  • What limitations are hidden by methods whose reproduced evidence is sparse or whose failure modes are not reported?
  • How well do skill and context-management methods generalize across applications, frameworks, and deployment domains?

system

Runnable GUI agent systems, frameworks, sandboxes, tool-use platforms, or orchestration layers

58 Methodology quality 99 Recency 38 Reproducibility 44 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Harness-native agent training and orchestration frameworks

End-to-end reinforcement learning or online policy optimization for agents embedded in complex inference harnesses or live browser environments, including scalable rollout isolation and trajectory collection.

Open edge: Error recovery remains weak after training; harness design appears to affect trainability; visible reproducibility artifacts and independent deployment evidence are limited.
Weakly supervised and diffusion-based GUI policy models

Train visual grounding and navigation policies with limited annotation or alternative decoding, targeting mobile, desktop, and web control.

Open edge: Cross-platform generalization, breadth of comparison against larger or proprietary systems, and unreported limitations remain unclear.
Reusable skill and memory layers for computer-use agents

Convert validated GUI trajectories or documented workflows into multimodal or callable artifacts to reduce token cost, execution time, and repeated planning.

Open edge: Deterministic replay applies only when preconditions match; ambiguous, incompatible, dynamic, or asynchronous UI steps are rejected or not fully evaluated.
Step-level diagnostics and safety benchmarks for GUI agents

Isolate transition understanding and adversarial safety in browser or coding settings beyond end-task success, including temporal ordering, action inference, and prompt injection.

Open edge: Offline and OS/action-vocabulary constraints limit ecological validity; optimized attack reproducibility and broader attack surfaces are not directly measured.

Shared Direction

  • The 2026 frontier treats GUI agents as runnable systems rather than static models, with closed-loop rollouts, task success, and cost or latency treated as first-class evaluation signals.
  • There is a shared effort to reduce reliance on expensive annotations and repeated planning by learning from unannotated screenshots, live interaction, or validated trajectories.
  • Evaluation is expanding from final task completion to include step-level transitions, failure diagnostics, and adversarial safety behavior.
  • These systems separate policy learning from complex harness or browser infrastructure through proxies, orchestrators, or containerized rollouts.

Key Differences

  • Learning signal differs: weak supervision from unannotated screenshots plus small calibration data, online multi-turn RL on live environments, supervised fine-tuning with diffusion decoding, or retrieval/replay without policy training.
  • Artifact and representation choices differ: visual-augmented skill libraries retain interface images, callable memories encode preconditions and payloads, while model-centric approaches encode behavior in weights.
  • Evaluation depth differs: some target end-to-end benchmark success, others target step-level temporal ordering and action recognition, and others target adversarial task completion under prompt injection.
  • Deployment setting differs: offline diagnostic instances cannot capture asynchronous live dynamics, while live-browser and containerized rollouts introduce different reproducibility and safety boundaries.

Open Questions

  • Can post-training RL improve error recovery, and how much does harness design limit trainability?
  • How do skill and memory systems generalize beyond deterministic replay to ambiguous, asynchronous, or cross-OS interactions?
  • Does safety hardening transfer across application domains, or is it inherently conditioned on task surface and optimization-based attacks?
  • What independent reproduction evidence exists given that several visible papers lack code, dataset, or artifact links beyond textual claims?

application

Domain applications of GUI agents, such as office automation, mobile apps, web tasks, software operation, or professional tools

63 Methodology quality 99 Recency 37 Reproducibility 49 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Real-world OS and desktop task benchmarking

Capturing heterogeneous GUI and CLI workflows across applications with deterministic, fine-grained sub-goal scoring instead of only final success.

Open edge: Coverage is limited to specific desktop, mobile, or short-video settings; transfer to unobserved applications and real user workloads is not established.
Multimodal skill and replay memory construction

Reusing validated trajectories or visual documentation to raise success and reduce token/latency cost on repeated computer-use tasks.

Open edge: Maintenance under UI drift, ambiguous preconditions, and cross-application generalization are not fully verified.
Dynamic, video-guided, and drag-based interaction grounding

Grounding agents beyond static click-only GUI actions, including moving short-video interfaces, keyframe-guided tasks, and drag manipulations.

Open edge: Observation control and generalization across dynamic interface types, especially beyond the studied short-video and drag domains, are missing.
Specialized vision-language GUI agent architectures

Training or pretraining multimodal models for GUI grounding and navigation with alternative decoding such as block-wise diffusion.

Open edge: Comparative evidence is limited to selected grounding/navigation benchmarks; robustness, interactive reliability, and deployment trade-offs remain underreported.

Shared Direction

  • Fine-grained evaluation beyond final success is needed; sub-goal completion, cost-accuracy, and token/latency efficiency reveal differences that Pass@1 alone hides.
  • Reusable multimodal skills or replay memories improve agent performance and efficiency over no-memory or text-only baselines.
  • Current GUI agents remain below human-level reliability on dynamic, drag-based, or mixed GUI/CLI tasks, with observation control and UI grounding being weak points.
  • Deterministic, rule-based or artifact-validated checks support reproducible benchmark evaluation.

Key Differences

  • Task focus differs substantially: static desktop workflows versus continuous living-screen or short-video dynamics, implying different state representations and observation requirements.
  • Reuse mechanisms differ: hierarchical multimodal skill library with visual figures versus compiled state-machine replay versus parameter-controlled callable memory.
  • Improvement strategy differs: some works change data or fine-tuning, others change runtime memory/frameworks, and another changes the generative model architecture.
  • Evaluation objective differs: passing tasks, minimizing cost/time, or improving grounding/navigation scores are not treated as one common objective.

Open Questions

  • Do gains on the studied benchmarks transfer to real office, mobile, or professional workflows outside the benchmark applications and platforms?
  • How should skill and replay memories be invalidated or repaired when application UIs, workflows, or user goals change?
  • How can observation control be explicitly modeled and evaluated beyond short-video cost-accuracy settings?
  • What is the cross-benchmark generalization of drag-based and video-guided grounding when combined with memory, skill, or diffusion-based agent backbones?
  • The packet lacks code, dataset, and limitation details for several methods; which reported improvements are reproducible across independent deployments?

Research Lines

Taxonomy and roadmap synthesis

Maps fragmented GUI-agent directions into research gaps and priority paths.

Open edge: Whether the synthesis is backed by systematic evidence rather than selective method or benchmark examples.
Comparative evaluation and benchmark design

Creates controlled tasks and quantitative metrics for web, mobile, and video-guided GUI agents.

Open edge: Cross-setting result compatibility, multi-step trajectory coverage, and target-specific recalibration.
Verifiable task synthesis and reinforcement learning

Scales training signal and rollout efficiency through verifiable tasks and online RL.

Open edge: Benchmark-to-real deployment bias and dependence on verifier or reward calibration.
GUI grounding and uncertainty estimation

Quantifies reliability of GUI grounding and builds spatial safety mechanisms such as conformal click regions.

Open edge: Transfer across model tiers and interfaces, and calibration under distribution shift.

Shared Direction

  • Progress is mostly presented through paired benchmark-method contributions with quantitative success, ranking, or safety metrics.
  • Autonomous or verifiable evaluation signals are widely used but treated as fallible and in need of correction or domain-specific revalidation.
  • Generalization claims rely on transfer to out-of-distribution settings, yet the evidence shows selective rather than uniform transfer.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation targets differ: single-step grounding and video-guided tasks versus multi-step desktop, mobile, fraud-detection, and safety red-teaming episodes.
  • Supervision and training signals differ: verifiable task completion, noisy autonomous reward, supervised trajectories, or human demonstrations.
  • Representation and control choices differ: keyframe extraction, structured context actions, uncertainty-score families, and tutorial graphs.
  • Deployment settings differ: open-weight versus closed-source models, and browser, coding, mobile, and desktop interfaces.

Open Questions

  • Which benchmark-to-benchmark results are compatible enough for aggregate synthesis, and which require target-specific recalibration or reranking?
  • What systematic taxonomy or roadmap conclusions can be drawn from the bounded evidence when explicit survey texts are not visible?
  • How do synthetic or verifiable tasks and autonomous reward signals transfer from benchmark-style computer-use settings to real-world GUI workflows?
  • Which context, uncertainty, and safety mechanisms remain robust under distribution shift and beyond the tested task families?
  • Which missing limitation, code, dataset, and metric details materially affect reproducibility and should be recorded as verification gaps?
