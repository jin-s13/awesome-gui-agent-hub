Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Real-world desktop and cross-application task suites

Makes GUI+CLI computer-use ability comparable across applications and frameworks through rule-based multi-checkpoint scoring.

Open edge: Coverage across OS versions, hardware, and application workflows; deterministic scoring may miss semantic success or user preferences.
Dynamic and video-guided interface benchmarks

Measures agents on content that changes between actions and on video-guided interactive tasks.

Open edge: Evidence is limited to short-video/browser or selected video settings; generalization to other dynamic interfaces and long-horizon tasks remains untested.
Fine-grained grounding, cost-accuracy, and uncertainty evaluation

Probes UI grounding, observation efficiency, and confidence calibration instead of only final task accuracy.

Open edge: Single-step uncertainty evidence does not cover multi-step agent trajectories; cross-model and cross-interface ranking transfer is weak.
Benchmark-driven self-improvement and training methods

Uses OSWorld, ScienceBoard, CUA-World, and related suites to improve frozen or open agents via harness edits, RL, weak supervision, or verifiable task synthesis.

Open edge: Task synthesis and harness optimization may overfit benchmark surfaces; transfer and data requirements beyond tested suites need more evidence.

Shared Direction

  • Executable environment fidelity matters: studies converge on real/desktop/browser settings rather than only static screenshots.
  • Aggregate success is insufficient: multi-checkpoint, cost-accuracy, sub-goal, and uncertainty/calibration metrics reveal differences hidden by Pass@1.
  • Frontier GUI agents still show observable capability gaps, especially in observation control, grounding transfer, and generalization beyond tested interfaces.
  • Training, harness, or skill interventions can improve the same frozen backbone, so evaluation conclusions are sensitive to the agent wrapper and environment.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation target differs: single-step GUI grounding, multi-step desktop workflows, and dynamic/video-guided screens are treated separately.
  • Intervention approach differs: retrieve multimodal skills, optimize the execution harness, train with weak supervision or online RL, or synthesize verifiable tasks.
  • Metric priority differs: deterministic rule-based scoring, cost-accuracy efficiency, uncertainty ranking transfer, and SOTA success rates are emphasized differently.
  • Deployment setting differs: real macOS desktop, browser-simulated platforms, video-guided tasks, or containerized OSWorld/ScienceBoard tasks.

Open Questions

  • Do current benchmark task distributions cover the settings where computer-use agents are expected to help, such as long-horizon workflows, OS/version drift, and permission-restricted apps?
  • Which fine-grained metrics best predict downstream user utility and safe deployment when Pass@1, cost-accuracy, and uncertainty rankings may not transfer across models or interfaces?
  • How stable are benchmark conclusions across implementation harnesses, compute budgets, and repeated runs, especially when harness optimization yields large gains on frozen models?
  • Can uncertainty quantification extend beyond single-step grounding to multi-step GUI trajectories and dynamic screens without target-specific re-ranking?
  • Where do missing artifacts and baseline details limit direct comparison, and how should reporting standards separate methodology evidence from popularity or recency signals?

Research Lines

Benchmark and evaluation design for GUI capabilities

Captures sub-goal completion, information efficiency, interaction-type coverage, and grounding reliability that aggregate success rates miss.

Open edge: Coverage is bounded to specific platforms or interaction types; some limitation fields are missing; multi-step reliability is not fully evaluated.
Data-efficient training and self-improvement methods

Reduces dependence on dense human annotations by using large unannotated screenshots, verifiable synthesized tasks, or evidence-driven harness edits around frozen models.

Open edge: Generalization beyond reported benchmarks is not established; gains may depend on harness, task synthesis quality, or calibration data; method contribution versus surrounding system choices is not fully isolated.
Observation and action-grounding methods for dynamic or non-click interfaces

Addresses changing screen content, video frame selection, and drag interactions beyond click-only grounding.

Open edge: Observation control failure remains unsolved; evidence is limited to short-video platforms and selected drag categories; broader dynamic interface types are unverified.
Uncertainty and reliability estimation for grounding

Ranks and calibrates post-hoc uncertainty for grounding and creates spatial conformal click regions for safety.

Open edge: Transfer of UQ rankings across model tiers and interfaces is weak; evaluation is single-step only; coverage degrades under distribution shift, and training-time methods are not examined.

Shared Direction

  • Fine-grained evaluation reveals capability differences that aggregate success or pass rates hide, such as sub-goal progress, information cost, and action-type competence.
  • Research is converging on reducing annotation cost and model retraining: methods exploit unannotated screenshots, synthesized verifiable tasks, repeated-run evidence, and frozen-harness edits.
  • Dynamic or non-click grounding remains a weak spot, with evidence showing failures in observation control, drag action coverage, and uncertainty transfer.

Key Differences

  • Intervention point differs: model-parameter training or fine-tuning versus frozen-model harness evolution, input keyframe selection, or post-hoc uncertainty calibration.
  • Evaluation target differs: static desktop goal completion, dynamic cost-accuracy, video-guided task success, drag-based manipulation, or single-step grounding reliability define different capability notions.
  • Supervision and verification differ: unannotated screenshots with small annotated calibration, multi-agent synthesized verifiable tasks, repeated-run evidence, and post-hoc benchmark-based UQ adopt distinct signals.
  • Generalization strategy differs: some studies aim for cross-platform or frozen-harness transfer, while others remain within a single platform or action family, changing which risks are tested.

Open Questions

  • What observation-control mechanism would prevent over- and under-observation in dynamic interfaces?
  • How far do harness, training-efficiency, and uncertainty methods transfer across model tiers, operating systems, applications, and interaction modes?
  • Can evaluation metrics isolate model capability from framework skill libraries, harness code, and input-preprocessing choices?
  • How should uncertainty calibration extend beyond single-step grounding to full multi-step agent trajectories?
  • What evaluation can cover the under-tested boundary of dynamic video, drag, and GUI+CLI interactions in a common protocol?

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Task abstraction and interaction taxonomy (e.g., MacAgentBench, DragOn, LivingScreen)

Turns GUI-agent competence into measurable task suites for desktop, drag-based, and dynamic short-video interfaces, with executable or rule-based checks.

Open edge: Pass@1 can hide subgoal gaps; platform-specific findings and empty limitation fields leave cross-domain validity uncertain.
External skill and harness improvement (e.g., VISUALSKILL, GUI-HARVEST)

Improves frozen backbone agents by adding multimodal visual skills or evolving the execution harness from action-and-screenshot evidence.

Open edge: Skill artifacts appear application-specific; cross-benchmark transfer evidence exists mainly for harness optimization, and limitation documentation is missing.
Scalable supervision and verifiable RL (e.g., ScaleCUA, GUICrafter)

Lowers annotation cost and supports online RL via unannotated visual grounding or synthesized verifiable tasks with frontier sampling.

Open edge: Verification environments may not cover real-world GUI variability; reported gains are largely benchmark-specific and limitation evidence is sparse.
Uncertainty quantification and reliability framing (e.g., Argus)

Benchmarks post-hoc uncertainty signals for GUI grounding, measuring ranking stability and conformal click-region calibration.

Open edge: Evidence is limited to single-step grounding; cross-model and cross-interface ranking transfer is weak, and coverage degrades under distribution shift.

Shared Direction

  • Executable or rule-based verification is the dominant way to turn GUI task success into a measurable signal.
  • Single Pass@1-style success hides capability differences; finer subgoal, cost-accuracy, or uncertainty metrics are needed.
  • Improving agents without full backbone retraining via external skills, harness changes, or weak supervision is a shared frontier direction.
  • Scale and verifiability of training/evaluation data are treated as central levers for GUI-agent capability.

Key Differences

  • Representation: static screenshot/click/drag abstractions coexist with dynamic living-screen interfaces requiring observation control.
  • Supervision and adaptation route: some approaches update policies through weak supervision or online RL, while others keep models frozen and modify skills or harness.
  • Evaluation target: success rate, subgoal completion, information efficiency, and uncertainty calibration are optimized differently.
  • Deployment setting: desktop macOS, short-video platforms, drag interactions, and single-step grounding lead to different assumptions about interface stability.

Open Questions

  • Do current task abstractions transfer to long-horizon, cross-application workflows outside their original platforms?
  • Which uncertainty signals remain stable across model tiers and interfaces, and can calibration survive distribution shift?
  • Can verifiable RL task synthesis preserve enough real-world diversity to avoid overfitting to docker or benchmark environments?
  • What evidence links fine-grained subgoal metrics to downstream reliability, safety, and user-facing task completion?

system

Runnable GUI agent systems, frameworks, sandboxes, tool-use platforms, or orchestration layers

59 Methodology quality 99 Recency 39 Reproducibility 43 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

End-to-end trained GUI/computer-use policies

Trains a multimodal policy to ground screenshots and produce actions, reducing human annotation or improving task success across desktop, mobile, and web benchmarks.

Open edge: Error recovery remains weak after RL; cross-platform transfer and scalability beyond reported benchmarks are not independently verified; many abstracts omit explicit limitations.
Harness-native RL and orchestration frameworks

Turns deployed agent harnesses into trainable rollouts by intercepting model calls and running stateful multi-process inference in isolated environments.

Open edge: Harness design strongly influences trainability; some abilities such as recovery remain weak; reproducibility signals such as code and dataset availability are incomplete.
Skill and memory layers over GUI agents

Converts validated trajectories or authored documentation into reusable multimodal skills or replayable memories to cut token cost, time, and grounding errors.

Open edge: Memory reuse is limited to deterministic or compatible runtime states; skill construction is per-application and needs coverage beyond tested applications.
Diagnostic evaluation for recovery and personalization

Measures when and why agents fail on recovery consistency or user-preference execution rather than only aggregate task completion.

Open edge: Current agents show large personalization drops and fail to exploit latent recovery; ideal recovery is simulated or condition-specific, and long-horizon replay cost is unresolved.

Shared Direction

  • GUI/computer-use agents need visual grounding and environment-grounded evaluation on realistic interactive tasks.
  • External runtime layers—training loops, skill libraries, memory, or evaluation harnesses—can improve task success or efficiency without necessarily retraining the base model.
  • Reported gains are usually measured against task-success benchmarks and matched baselines, with error recovery, personalization, and adaptability remaining weak across systems.
  • Executable, reproducible artifacts are still unevenly available; missing code, dataset, or limitation details are verification gaps rather than proof of absence.

Key Differences

  • Whether to train an end-to-end policy over a unified action space or keep the base agent fixed and add external skills, memory, or RL proxies.
  • Whether supervision should come from unannotated screenshots with weak labels, hybrid application-artifact supervision, or online RL in deployed harnesses.
  • Whether evaluation should target aggregate benchmark success or diagnostic failure modes such as recovery consistency and preference execution.
  • Whether the agent should emit GUI-only actions or a combined GUI-plus-code action space, and whether generation should be diffusion-based or follow standard LLM decoding.

Open Questions

  • Can post-training or memory layers reduce error recovery failures that remain weak even after RL training?
  • Do skill and replayable-memory systems generalize beyond deterministic, per-application or benchmark-specific conditions?
  • Can weak supervision from unannotated screenshots scale to broad computer-use tasks while maintaining the reported data-efficiency gains?
  • How should personalization and recovery be evaluated independently from overall task success, and which metrics best capture user-specific execution?

application

Domain applications of GUI agents, such as office automation, mobile apps, web tasks, software operation, or professional tools

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Real-world GUI benchmark and interaction suites

Defines task sets and metrics for desktop, drag, dynamic short-video, and video-guided GUI interactions to expose failure modes beyond final success.

Open edge: Coverage is platform- or interaction-specific; dynamic environments still exceed model cost-accuracy, and some benchmarks lack clear code, data, or limitation evidence.
Skill, memory, and replay reuse for computer-use agents

Improves accuracy or latency by retrieving multimodal skills or compiling successful runs into screen-checked state machines for repeated tasks.

Open edge: Transfer across applications, platforms, and task variants, plus robustness under screen-state mismatch, are not established; visual, text, and programmatic artifacts lack unified comparison.
End-to-end model training and unified action spaces for GUI agents

Trains multimodal agents to generate GUI actions, and in some cases unifies GUI and code execution, reducing dependence on brittle tool composition.

Open edge: Real-world generality beyond reported benchmarks, cross-platform transfer, and the boundary between unified training and modular tool or skill composition remain open.
Observation and interaction grounding beyond click

Represents drag operations, dynamic content, and video-guided tasks using datasets, keyframes, or browser-based dynamic environments to improve visual grounding and action coverage.

Open edge: Current agents over- or under-observe dynamic screens; keyframe extraction gains are shown mainly on VideoQA or agentic benchmarks, not broad professional applications.

Shared Direction

  • Fine-grained evaluation beyond final success is necessary because agents with similar pass rates differ in sub-goal or interaction-level behavior.
  • Reusable task artifacts, whether multimodal skills, compiled state-machine programs, or extracted keyframes, improve accuracy or latency over one-shot generation.
  • Current frontier agents still lack robust observation and interaction control for dynamic screens, drag actions, and video-guided tasks.
  • End-to-end training or unified action spaces can improve GUI performance compared with simple tool-composition or no-skill baselines.

Key Differences

  • Some methods store multimodal visual-plus-text skills, while others compile successful runs into executable state machines or extract task-relevant keyframes; these reflect different assumptions about which artifact should persist.
  • Some approaches adapt through end-to-end training and online reinforcement learning, while others adapt at inference via retrieval or replay, implying different supervision costs and generalization expectations.
  • Evaluation settings differ substantially: real desktop GUI+CLI tasks, browser-based short-video dynamics, drag interactions, video-guided tasks, and cross-platform navigation or grounding benchmarks.
  • Success criteria differ: final success, fine-grained checkpoints, cost-accuracy or information efficiency, latency speedup, and relative benchmark gains all complicate direct comparison.
  • Action-space design differs: some methods output GUI clicks or coordinates, others unify GUI actions with code execution, and others focus on drag or observation control rather than click-only actions.

Open Questions

  • How well do skill libraries and compiled replay programs transfer across applications, operating systems, and changed screen states without brittle fallback?
  • Can agents learn observation control policies that reduce over- and under-observation on dynamic short-video or live interfaces?
  • Do improvements on video-guided keyframes, drag grounding, and OS-level benchmarks translate to professional or office workflows outside the evaluated platforms?
  • What is the right boundary between end-to-end trained agents and modular retrieval or replay components when action spaces include both GUI manipulation and code execution?
  • Several top papers lack explicit limitation sections or code and dataset signals, so reproducibility and negative results remain verification gaps.

survey

Surveys, taxonomies, tutorials, position papers, or roadmap papers for GUI agents

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Controlled evaluation and gap mapping

Turns GUI-agent claims into measurable failure modes for grounding, safety, fraud detection, uncertainty, and reproducibility through controlled benchmarks and audit protocols.

Open edge: Each benchmark isolates a narrow setting; cross-interface, cross-model, and cross-attack transfer is often weak, and no sampled evidence provides systematic priority ranking across gaps.
Dense supervision and verifiable task synthesis

Addresses data and task scarcity for training computer-use agents using controlled desktop environments, dense element annotations, large-scale synthetic corpora, and verifiable RL task generation.

Open edge: Transfer from synthetic or controlled environments to unseen real-world applications and long-horizon reliability is only partially demonstrated; distribution shift and application-state coverage remain unresolved.
Harness, context, and policy optimization

Improves frozen or small models by optimizing the executable harness, managing long-horizon context proactively, or allocating rollouts efficiently instead of retraining full backbones.

Open edge: Evidence is concentrated on OSWorld, ScienceBoard, mobile, and related benchmarks; broader cross-benchmark generalization and failure boundaries need more independent verification.
Video-guided task grounding

Bridges video understanding and GUI-agent actions by extracting task-relevant keyframes and evaluating MLLM-based agents on video-guided interactive tasks.

Open edge: Keyframe benefits are shown on video QA and GUI task benchmarks, but integration with multi-step agent trajectories and broader GUI environments is not established.

Shared Direction

  • There is convergence on treating GUI-agent evaluation as task completion or failure-mode measurement in controlled environments.
  • Multiple works improve pretrained agents through supervision, context, harness, or rollout changes rather than only scaling model size.
  • Safety, uncertainty, and reproducibility are emerging as first-class evaluation concerns, not auxiliary checks.
  • Benchmark-specific metrics such as task success, grounding accuracy, attack success rate, and ranking stability are standard, with held-out or transfer tests used to probe generalization.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation target differs: some work measures single-step grounding or uncertainty calibration, while other work measures long-horizon task completion, fraud judgment, or safety attack success.
  • Supervision mode differs: dense environment annotations, verifiable RL task synthesis, repeated-run harness edits, and human-authored attack templates emphasize different failure signals.
  • Interaction surface differs: desktop, mobile, web, SMS-to-webpage, coding, and video-guided tasks imply different observation and action spaces.
  • Generalization claims differ: some papers report cross-benchmark transfer, while others report weak transfer across model tiers or domains and recommend target-specific revalidation.
  • Reproducibility evidence differs: some papers provide code, dataset, or artifact signals; others provide only paper-level evidence and lack limitation details, making verification gaps harder to compare.

Open Questions

  • Which benchmark-specific improvements transfer to real-world GUI workflows beyond the tested desktop, mobile, web, coding, SMS, and video-guided settings?
  • How should survey and taxonomy work prioritize gaps among grounding, long-horizon memory, safety, uncertainty, and data synthesis without systematic coverage evidence?
  • Under what conditions do uncertainty rankings or safety hardening transfer across model tiers and interface domains?
  • Can dense synthetic supervision or verifiable task synthesis maintain benefit under distribution shift and unseen application-state variation?
  • What missing code, dataset, metric, baseline, and limitation details currently hide reproducibility gaps in the visible frontier?
