Research Lines

Real-world desktop and OS task construction

Provides executable macOS and OS tasks with deterministic rule-based scoring and fine-grained sub-goal checkpoints.

Open edge: Coverage across apps, OS versions, and dynamic workflows is not established; limitation evidence is often absent.
Dynamic and video-guided benchmark environments

Introduces changing interfaces and video-guided tasks to test observation control and information efficiency.

Open edge: LivingScreen is limited to short-video platforms; video-guided benchmark limitation details are missing, so broader dynamic-interface coverage is unverified.
Uncertainty and metric stress-testing

Benchmarks post-hoc uncertainty estimation and spatial calibration for GUI grounding across many models and datasets.

Open edge: Evidence covers single-step grounding, not multi-step trajectories; cross-model and cross-interface transfer is weak and coverage degrades under shift.
Agent and harness improvement on benchmark suites

Uses skill libraries, harness optimization, verifiable task synthesis, and weak supervision to improve frozen or open agents on benchmark tasks.

Open edge: Transfer evidence is limited to selected suites; most limitation sections are empty, so robustness and boundary conditions remain open.

Shared Direction

  • Executable benchmark tasks are evaluated across several open and proprietary models, with deterministic or verifiable scoring as the main target.
  • Aggregate success is treated as insufficient; fine-grained sub-goal, cost-accuracy, observation, and uncertainty diagnostics are used to reveal hidden failure modes.
  • Current agents remain below human-level in dynamic or multi-step settings and show weak cross-setting transfer or calibration.
  • Several papers provide code, dataset, or artifact signals, but missing limitation sections leave methodology boundaries unverified.

Key Differences

  • They evaluate different deployment settings: desktop macOS GUI+CLI, browser-based short-video, video-guided GUI interaction, containerized OS tasks, and single-step GUI grounding.
  • They optimize different levers: benchmark design, multimodal skill retrieval, execution-harness editing, verifiable RL task synthesis, weakly-supervised grounding, or uncertainty calibration.
  • Metric emphasis varies from Pass@1 and sub-goal completion to cost-accuracy information efficiency and uncertainty-ranking or coverage.
  • Some contributions are benchmark-only observations, while others directly change agent training or execution, making comparability across contributions indirect.

Open Questions

  • Can fine-grained and uncertainty diagnostics be standardized so benchmark conclusions transfer without target-specific reranking?
  • Do harness, skill, and RL gains persist across unseen OS versions, dynamic interfaces, and longer multi-step workflows?
  • Which proxy metrics best align with user decision quality when observation cost, calibration shift, and execution variability are present?
  • What reporting and artifact requirements are needed given that several limitation sections are absent and reproducibility signals are uneven?

method

GUI agent models, planning, grounding, action prediction, training, or reasoning methods

61 Methodology quality 100 Recency 38 Reproducibility 44 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Real-world GUI agent benchmarking and task design

Measures VLM agent capability on macOS desktop, dynamic short-video, and drag-based interactions, exposing gaps in observation control, skill use, and action coverage.

Open edge: Evidence is concentrated in specific platforms and interaction types; cost-accuracy, sub-goal metrics, and cross-benchmark transfer are not yet standardized.
Training and self-improvement methods for GUI agents

Reduces annotation cost and improves success through weak supervision, verifiable task synthesis, online RL, or harness optimization around frozen backbone models.

Open edge: Attribution is hard because gains may come from the base model, framework, skill library, or harness; transfer beyond OSWorld, WindowsAgentArena, and ScienceBoard is underevidenced.
Visual grounding and input representation for GUI control

Handles video-guided tasks, drag grounding, and grounded uncertainty by extracting keyframes, covering drag interactions, and calibrating click regions.

Open edge: Observation control under dynamic content, cross-model and cross-interface UQ transfer, and multi-step grounding remain unresolved.

Shared Direction

  • Current GUI agent work treats VLM-based agents as the default decision model and evaluates them through environment-grounded task completion rather than only static UI understanding.
  • Papers agree that real-world and dynamic GUI tasks expose failures not visible in static click-only benchmarks, especially observation strategy, execution variability, and action primitive coverage.
  • Multiple methods move from manual annotation toward scalable supervision or self-improvement, using screenshots, execution traces, verifiable task synthesis, or harness edits.
  • Evaluation increasingly uses deterministic checks, fine-grained sub-goal scoring, or cost-accuracy trade-offs to separate pass/fail from capability.

Key Differences

  • Some work targets better measurement through benchmarks, while other work targets better behavior through training, RL, or harness optimization.
  • Supervision assumptions differ: unannotated screenshots plus small calibrated data, verifiable synthesized tasks, frozen-model harness edits, and fine-tuning on drag-specific data are competing routes.
  • Interaction scope differs across click grounding, GUI+CLI tasks, short-video dynamic content, and drag actions, so findings do not automatically transfer across interfaces.
  • Evaluation targets differ from single-step grounding with uncertainty or conformal regions to multi-step desktop task completion, changing which failure modes are visible.
  • Closed-source models require different UQ or adaptation strategies because internal states are inaccessible, while open-weight methods allow hidden-state or density-based approaches.

Open Questions

  • How much of reported gains on OSWorld-like or desktop benchmarks comes from the proposed training or harness method rather than skill libraries, framework choices, or backbone model capability?
  • Can observation control be learned or built into agents to reduce over- and under-observation on dynamic screens without losing task accuracy?
  • Do drag-based and GUI+CLI grounding improvements generalize to downstream multi-step computer-use tasks beyond the studied benchmark?
  • How should uncertainty quantification be selected or adapted when models and interfaces change, given weak cross-tier UQ ranking transfer?
  • What evidence would establish transfer across operating systems, application sets, and interaction modalities beyond the studied macOS, short-video, drag, and OSWorld-style environments?

theory

Formal analysis, task abstractions, interaction theory, or reliability/safety framing for GUI agents

61 Methodology quality 100 Recency 38 Reproducibility 44 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Interaction coverage and task abstraction benchmarks

Expands evaluation beyond static clicking by formalizing composite GUI+CLI desktop tasks, dynamic living-screen interactions, and drag-based manipulation with rule-based or browser-based scoring.

Open edge: Multi-step trajectory evaluation, observation-control formalization, cross-platform migration, and evidence that benchmark coverage predicts real deployment behavior.
External scaffolding: skill libraries and harness self-improvement

Improves frozen agents by adding hierarchical multimodal skills or automatically optimizing the execution harness from screenshot-grounded failure evidence.

Open edge: Whether gains transfer across applications and benchmarks without per-target re-optimization, and how much comes from framework design versus backbone model ability.
Data-efficient and verifiable training for GUI policies

Reduces annotation cost through weak supervision from unannotated screenshots or verifiable task synthesis and online RL, scaling open agents toward stronger completion.

Open edge: Distribution-shift robustness and whether synthetic or weakly supervised tasks predict failures and transfers to real GUI environments.
Reliability, uncertainty, and safety-oriented evaluation

Adds fine-grained sub-goal metrics, cost-accuracy, post-hoc uncertainty quantification, and conformal click regions to surface failure modes beyond pass/fail.

Open edge: Multi-step uncertainty guarantees, cross-model and cross-interface UQ transfer, and coverage under calibration/test or interface mismatch.

Shared Direction

  • Evaluation should include finer signals than overall success because similar Pass@1 or task accuracy can hide different sub-goal completion, observation efficiency, or grounding reliability.
  • Frozen model performance can be improved by external scaffolding such as skills, harness edits, or tools without retraining the backbone.
  • Dynamic interfaces and non-click interactions expose substantial gaps in current agents, especially observation control and drag-related grounding.
  • Unannotated screenshots and verifiable task synthesis can reduce dependence on costly human annotations.

Key Differences

  • Where improvement should occur: some work updates the model through online RL or weak supervision, while others keep the model frozen and change the skill library or execution harness.
  • Which evaluation target matters most: task completion, information efficiency, uncertainty-ranking transfer, or drag-action grounding; each selects different primary metrics.
  • Whether visual artifacts in external knowledge are necessary; one line shows multimodal skills outperform matched text-only skills, while others optimize code-level or harness-level changes.
  • How broadly uncertainty rankings transfer; evidence shows within-model transfer can be strong but cross-tier or cross-interface transfer is weak and target-specific reranking is needed.

Open Questions

  • Can post-hoc uncertainty quantification for GUI grounding be extended from single-step grounding to multi-step agent trajectories with valid coverage?
  • How should observation control be formalized and improved for dynamic living-screen interfaces where over-observation and under-observation are dominant failures?
  • Do skill-library and harness optimizations generalize across applications, platforms, and benchmarks without re-optimization or fine-grained re-validation?
  • Under what distribution shifts do verifiable task synthesis and weakly supervised GUI training remain reliable and safe enough for deployment?

system

Runnable GUI agent systems, frameworks, sandboxes, tool-use platforms, or orchestration layers

59 Methodology quality 99 Recency 38 Reproducibility 44 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

GUI skill and memory reuse for computer-use agents, for example VISUALSKILL and EchoPath

Encodes validated application workflows or multimodal skill artifacts so agents can reuse procedures, reduce token and execution time, and improve UI grounding at runtime.

Open edge: Reuse depends on deterministic applicability or per-application authoring; evidence does not yet show generalization across applications, operating systems, live timing, or long-term artifact maintenance.
Harness-native and online training frameworks, for example OpenForgeRL, OpenWebRL, and GUICrafter

Trains GUI agents in real or harness-wrapped environments while reducing annotation cost through weak supervision, online multi-turn reinforcement learning, or interception of deployed harness calls.

Open edge: Error recovery remains weak after training; harness design affects learnability; evaluations use hundreds to thousands of tasks, leaving cross-domain and cross-infrastructure generalization uncertain.
Agent model architectures for GUI generation, for example LLaDA-UI

Provides an alternative vision-language GUI generation paradigm based on block-wise diffusion while aiming to preserve parallel decoding and improve grounding and navigation.

Open edge: Available evidence compares against selected Qwen baselines on six benchmarks and lacks limitation analysis on deployment latency, robustness, and broader reproducibility.
Diagnostic benchmarks and evaluation environments, for example Desktop-Delta Bench and OSWorld-Science

Evaluates computer-use agents beyond end-task success by testing GUI transition reconstruction, state verification, source tracking, action inference, and scientific software workflows with execution-based scoring.

Open edge: Offline diagnostics do not capture asynchronous live dynamics; coverage is limited to Linux desktop action types or selected scientific domains, so real-time and cross-OS validity remains uncertain.

Shared Direction

  • Runnable GUI systems benefit from separating the agent policy from the harness or orchestration layer so one policy can be reused across environments.
  • Training and evaluation increasingly rely on execution-level or trajectory-level success signals instead of only measuring single-frame action localization.
  • Memory, skill, or replay artifacts can reduce inference cost and improve grounding when they preserve visual and contextual information and are applied under checked preconditions.
  • Weakly supervised or online reinforcement learning can substantially reduce dependence on large annotated GUI datasets but requires careful initialization and success judging.

Key Differences

  • Supervision strategy differs: some use massive unannotated screenshots with a small high-quality calibration set, while others use live online multi-turn RL or SFT plus RL, implying different data efficiency and infrastructure costs.
  • Representation differs: block-wise diffusion for GUI generation versus harness-wrapped autoregressive or multimodal policies, with no matched training and deployment comparison in the visible evidence.
  • Memory formulation differs: multimodal skill libraries organized by topic versus execution-level replayable trajectory memories with deterministic applicability checks and image-based reaiming.
  • Evaluation target differs: offline step-level transition diagnostics and domain-specific scientific software tasks versus live web or desktop task success, making reported results non-interchangeable.

Open Questions

  • Can reusable memory and skill artifacts remain correct as applications, OS versions, and UI layouts change, and what validation or maintenance do they require?
  • How can live GUI timing, asynchronous events, and error recovery be incorporated into training and offline diagnostics without simplifying the deployment setting?
  • What minimum supervision and infrastructure budget is required to achieve strong GUI-agent performance across web, desktop, mobile, and scientific software environments?
  • Do available code, dataset, benchmark, and harness artifacts support independent reproduction, and are missing limitation or artifact details verification gaps?

application

Domain applications of GUI agents, such as office automation, mobile apps, web tasks, software operation, or professional tools

64 Methodology quality 99 Recency 38 Reproducibility 48 Topical relevance

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Real-world GUI benchmarking across desktop, web, mobile, and dynamic media

Provides application-grounded task suites with rule-based, sub-goal, grounding, or cost-accuracy metrics for comparing GUI agents.

Open edge: Coverage remains uneven across platforms and interaction primitives; dynamic observation control and real-world reliability are still unresolved.
Multimodal skill and procedural knowledge libraries

Encodes workflows and visual references into hierarchical multimodal skills or compiled state-machine programs to improve accuracy or repeated-task speed.

Open edge: Transfer across applications, updates, and non-repeated tasks is not demonstrated; visual-skill evidence is limited to reported benchmarks.
Large-scale grounding datasets and model training recipes

Improves element grounding and navigation by pairing large screenshot/referring-expression datasets with supervised fine-tuning, D2RL, or diffusion-based VLM training.

Open edge: Cross-platform and cross-scale generalization, and the relative contribution of data versus training algorithm, remain open.
Dynamic and video GUI perception with observation control

Extracts task-relevant visual evidence from dynamic or video interfaces and measures information efficiency.

Open edge: Identified over- and under-observation failure lacks a generalized solution beyond short-video and video-guided benchmarks.

Shared Direction

  • Application-grounded evaluation increasingly reports sub-goal progression, grounding accuracy, or information cost rather than only final success, revealing failures hidden by coarse pass rates.
  • Domain-specific visual or procedural grounding helps: retaining visual figures, collecting drag or screenshot grounding data, and fine-tuning with application data improve agent performance.
  • Frontier GUI agents still trail human-like reliability in dynamic media, drag interactions, and multitasking observation control.
  • Desktop, web, mobile platforms, and GUI+CLI interactions are being treated as connected agent capability surfaces, not isolated evaluation tags.

Key Differences

  • Improvement strategy differs: inference-time skills or replay, training-time grounding datasets and D2RL, or model-level diffusion decoding.
  • Evaluation target differs: final Pass@1, sub-goal completion, grounding accuracy, cost-accuracy/information efficiency, or repeated-task speedup.
  • Interaction mode differs: static desktop/CLI, dynamic short-video observation, drag manipulations, video-guided tasks, and repeated workflows.
  • Representation and supervision differ: multimodal visual-text skills versus text-only skills, deterministic rule-based scoring versus benchmark-specific metrics, and fine-tuning versus compiled state-machine memory.

Open Questions

  • Can observation-control failures in dynamic interfaces be corrected while preserving task accuracy and information efficiency?
  • Do drag grounding gains from large interaction datasets transfer to downstream computer-use tasks beyond the held-out benchmark?
  • How robust are compiled replay programs to application UI updates and slight task wording changes?
  • What is the relative contribution of large grounding datasets versus D2RL-style training to cross-platform generalization?
  • Are multimodal skill libraries effective beyond the reported CUA-World and OSExpert-Eval settings, especially for professional tools and longer workflows?

Surveys, taxonomies, tutorials, position papers, or roadmap papers for GUI agents

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Benchmark-driven GUI evaluation and grounding protocols

Measures agent capability on controlled GUI grounding, video-guided, fraud-detection, safety, and uncertainty tasks while providing metrics and baselines.

Open edge: Coverage is limited to selected tasks and interfaces; closed-source or optimized adversarial settings are often not reproducible from the packet.
Data and verifiable task generation for computer-use training

Creates dense annotations or verifiable tasks to improve grounding and long-horizon policy learning without relying only on human demonstrations.

Open edge: Transfer to unseen applications and whether synthesized tasks match real-world distribution is only partially evidenced.
Execution harness and context management for frozen or compact policies

Improves agent reliability by optimizing the executable harness or by explicit memory actions while keeping or using small models.

Open edge: Benchmark breadth for long-horizon memory and cross-benchmark transfer remains narrow; implementation artifacts are inconsistently available.
Risk, calibration, and safety auditing

Evaluates uncertainty calibration, prompt-injection resistance, and security shortcuts under sanitized or controlled protocols.

Open edge: Findings are domain-conditioned and distribution-sensitive; coverage is limited and only some artifacts are public.

Shared Direction

  • GUI agent evaluation is fragmented across grounding, long-horizon completion, and risk dimensions; no single benchmark covers all relevant behaviors.
  • Frozen or compact models can be substantially improved by better data, context, or execution harness, indicating that non-model interventions are important.
  • Safety and uncertainty conclusions are highly sensitive to evaluation domain, interface, and available shortcuts.
  • Public artifacts and reproducibility signals are uneven, making cross-paper comparison reliant on summarized results rather than verified implementations.

Key Differences

  • Some work improves agents through large-scale verifiable task synthesis and online RL, while other work avoids model training and optimizes harness, context, or post-hoc calibration instead.
  • Evaluation targets differ: single-step grounding, long-horizon task completion, and safety or fraud judgment are not directly comparable, so state-of-the-art claims vary by setting.
  • Reproducibility evidence differs across contributions: some release detailed artifacts, while others mainly report benchmark scores, leaving verification gaps.

Open Questions

  • Which GUI-agent capabilities should a survey taxonomy prioritize when current primary evidence clusters on benchmarks and methods rather than systematic review?
  • How can long-horizon mobile, desktop, browser, and safety evaluations be aligned so performance improvements transfer across domains?
  • What minimum evidence of reproducibility and limitation reporting is needed to aggregate claims into a trustworthy comparative evaluation?
  • How do conclusions about uncertainty and prompt-injection safety change under optimized adversarial inputs and distribution shift?
