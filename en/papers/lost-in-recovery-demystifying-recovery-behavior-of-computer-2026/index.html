Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • ReTrace, a scalable evaluation framework for systematically studying recovery behavior in computer-use agents.
  • Formalization of Transition-Consistent Recovery: identical downstream actions from the recovered and target states must yield identical outcomes.
  • Operationalization of Transition-Consistent Recovery through goal-oriented action replay following controlled error injection.
  • First systematic study of autonomous, model-based recovery in computer-use agents.

Methodology

ReTrace is a scalable evaluation framework that injects controlled errors and then uses goal-oriented action replay to assess whether recovered states satisfy Transition-Consistent Recovery. It evaluates computer-use agents by checking whether identical downstream actions from recovered and target states yield identical outcomes.

Key Results

Using ReTrace, the paper reports that ideal recovery can unlock latent task completion capabilities, while current computer-use agents fail to exploit their latent recovery capabilities. It also finds that accurate model-based recovery yields measurable end-to-end gains.

Limitations

  • The abstract identifies limitations of existing recovery approaches rather than of ReTrace itself: environment resets are infeasible in proprietary settings, action replay is prohibitively expensive over long horizons, and API-based rollback is limited to web environments.
  • Model-based recovery was largely unexplored before ReTrace.
