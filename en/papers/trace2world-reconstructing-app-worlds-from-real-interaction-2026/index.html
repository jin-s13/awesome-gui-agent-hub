Trace2World reconstructs executable app worlds from real interaction traces, improving scalable GUI agent training and real-device performance.

Reasoning

The paper introduces a principled separation of behavior reconstruction from implementation, with strong empirical results including high workflow reproduction and real-device training gains. However, the abstract lacks details on limitations, reconstruction cost, and generalizability beyond the ten apps.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Introduces Trace2World, which separates global behavior reconstruction from implementation to avoid incorrect state transitions caused by coupled behavior inference and coding.
  • Reconciles interaction traces into a shared, source-linked app model and then compiles an executable environment.
  • Uses independent replay of source workflows to localize mismatches and enable bounded implementation repair.
  • Supports learner-adaptive task and environment augmentation constrained to source-supported behavior.
  • Introduces source-grounded validation that better preserves simulated training gains on real devices compared to self-validation.

Methodology

Trace2World constructs 50 executable GUI environments from 1,570 real interaction traces by reconciling traces into shared, source-linked app models and compiling them. Environments are validated through independent replay of source workflows to localize mismatches and guide bounded repair, then support learner-adaptive task and environment augmentation within source-supported behavior. Evaluation covers 200 workflows across ten apps, with comparisons to a task-local baseline and downstream GUI benchmarks plus real-device tasks.

Key Results

Trace2World reproduces 91.1% of 200 workflows end-to-end versus 59.8% for the strongest matched task-local baseline. Source-grounded validation retains 94.0% of simulated training gains on real devices versus 54.7% under self-validation; learner guidance raises benchmark success from 56.9% to 61.1%, and training improves performance by 20.2 points on the benchmark, 5.4-16.2 points across four external GUI benchmarks, and 16.3 points on 100 real-device tasks.

Limitations

  • Task and environment augmentation is limited to source-supported behavior.
  • Implementation repair is bounded rather than fully automatic or unbounded.
