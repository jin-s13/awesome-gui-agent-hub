Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Training-free LEASH framework for weak-to-strong escalation in computer-use agents, using latent uncertainty to decide when to hand off from a weak to a strong model.
  • Latent uncertainty estimation by comparing the weak model's hidden states to those from past successful and failed trajectories, with failure-like states signaling higher uncertainty.
  • Three-layer uncertainty mechanism: initial task uncertainty for early routing, step-level uncertainty for runtime escalation, and shadow observer for continued oversight after handoff to a black-box strong agent.
  • Uncertainty estimator reuses activations already produced during action generation, requiring no extra forward pass or dedicated monitor training.

Methodology

LEASH is a training-free framework where a weak agent acts by default and escalates to a strong agent based on latent uncertainty. It compares the weak model's hidden states against stored successful and failed trajectories to produce uncertainty signals, and adds a shadow observer for post-handoff oversight of the strong black-box agent. Evaluation is on OSWorld across four model backbones, comparing against standalone weak agents and random escalation, with metrics including task success, escalation rates, and inference cost.

Key Results

LEASH consistently improves over standalone weak agents and outperforms random escalation by 6.55-11.94 points at matched escalation rates. With Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, it achieves 73.66% task success at $0.4117 per task, matching always-on Claude Opus 5 (72.90%) while reducing inference cost by 19.2%.
