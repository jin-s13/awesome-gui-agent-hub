CUA-Recovery: Benchmarking Error Recovery in Long-Horizon Computer-Use Agents

Introduces CUA-Recovery, a benchmark with test and training suites for error detection/recovery in long-horizon computer-use agents, showing agents struggle to recognize and recover from errors.

The paper addresses an important gap by benchmarking error recovery rather than only final task success, using synthesized long-horizon desktop tasks and verified failure trajectories. Strengths include multiple agents evaluated and a large human-verified test suite; weaknesses include limited visible detail on fine-tuning outcomes and potential narrowness to desktop applications.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Introduces CUA-Recovery, a benchmark with human-verified test and training suites for evaluating error detection and recovery in long-horizon computer-use agents.
  • Proposes ReRail, a framework that synthesizes long-horizon tasks and verified recovery trajectories in a persistent desktop environment by composing MyPCBench tasks, collecting verifier-confirmed failures from multiple policies, and converting them into recovery trajectories.
  • Uses policy-induced, verifier-confirmed failures rather than manually injected errors to better reflect real agent failure types and horizons.
  • Defines an evaluation protocol using Error Awareness Rate and Pass@3, and shows that fine-tuning on recovery trajectories improves both recovery and clean-start task completion.

Methodology

CUA-Recovery is constructed with ReRail by composing MyPCBench tasks into long-horizon workflows with executable ground truth, collecting verifier-confirmed failures from multiple policies, and generating verified recovery trajectories. It contains a human-verified test suite of 2,250 erroneous states over 100 tasks and a training suite of 8.6K trajectories over 377 tasks. Evaluation uses six agents and six takeover depths with metrics including Error Awareness Rate and Pass@3, and fine-tunes Qwen3.5-35B-A3B on the training suite.

Key Results

Across six agents and six takeover depths, Error Awareness Rate reaches at most 44.0% and Pass@3 at most 25.5%. Fine-tuning Qwen3.5-35B-A3B raises average EAR from 19.9% to 35.2% and Rubric Score from 5.0% to 16.1% on held-out recovery states, with Pass@3 gains of 15.3 points on MyPCBench and 10.8 points on Odysseys.
