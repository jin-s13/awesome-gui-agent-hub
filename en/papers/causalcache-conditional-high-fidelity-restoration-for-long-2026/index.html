CausalCache: Conditional High-Fidelity Restoration for Long-Horizon GUI Agents

arXiv 2026 54.9 method

TLDR

CausalCache improves long-horizon GUI agents by reallocating visual-context budget to restore high-fidelity history screenshots, yielding gains on OSWorld-Verified and mobile benchmarks.

Reasoning

The paper introduces a novel conditional fidelity restoration method with a principled budget-reallocation mechanism and selective HGKV adapter, validated on multiple real-world benchmarks. Weaknesses include an abstract that appears truncated and limited clarity on baseline comparisons and statistical robustness.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Formulates conditional fidelity restoration for long-horizon GUI agents, where events persist in summary-only form linked to archived screenshots and a budget B limits how many are promoted to summary-plus-image form.
  • Introduces CausalCache, which reallocates the same visual-context budget over the complete trace by evicting a recent image only when a distant event has higher conditional marginal utility.
  • Proposes a history-gated key/value (HGKV) adapter that modifies only restored history-image tokens and is exactly bypassed when no history image is present.
  • Uses matched-budget replacement groups and per-arm-anchored difference-in-differences supervision so uniform history amplification has zero value, enabling a budget-aware selector to choose which summarized events to restore.
  • Evaluates with memory-critical splits fixed by benchmark metadata at construction and matched controls to isolate where memory restoration gains concentrate.

Methodology

CausalCache stores each interaction event as a summary linked to an archived screenshot and promotes up to B events to summary-plus-image form using a learned budget-aware selector trained with matched-budget replacement groups and per-arm-anchored difference-in-differences supervision. A history-gated key/value adapter injects only restored history-image tokens and is bypassed when no image is restored. The method is compared with Recent-B and summary-only baselines on desktop OSWorld-Verified and zero-shot on a cross-application mobile benchmark.

Key Results

On OSWorld-Verified, restoring history to high fidelity is worth about 13 success points over summary-only memory, while same-budget allocations remain indistinguishable. Zero-shot on the mobile benchmark, CausalCache improves full-roster success over the same-budget recent allocation by +3.7 points, with +8.6 points on the memory-critical split, no detectable effect on matched controls, and a significant split-by-method interaction.

Limitations

  • The frozen desktop policy shows no reliable preference for a task-relevant archived screenshot over the recent frame it would displace.
  • HGKV learns the selectivity only inside a pre-specified drift envelope.
  • On OSWorld-Verified, same-budget allocations remain indistinguishable, so reallocation choice alone shows no measured benefit there.
  • Zero-shot mobile gains show no detectable effect on matched controls, concentrating only in the memory-critical split.
