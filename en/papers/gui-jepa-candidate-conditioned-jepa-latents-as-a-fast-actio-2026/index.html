GUI-JEPA: Candidate-Conditioned JEPA Latents as a Fast Action Policy for Mobile GUI Agents

arXiv 2026 69.6 method

TLDR

GUI-JEPA introduces a decoder-free JEPA-based mobile GUI agent policy that scores candidate actions in latent space, achieving faster inference than fine-tuned 7B VLMs with fewer parameters.

Reasoning

The paper proposes a novel architecture that avoids token-by-token decoding and demonstrates clear speed/parameter advantages on AndroidControl. However, accuracy is 86-92% of a stronger baseline, and the abstract does not discuss broader generalization or failure cases.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • First decoder-free GUI agent policy built on a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), selecting actions in latent space instead of decoding action text token-by-token.
  • Action- and region-conditioned latent predictor that turns a screen latent into one latent per candidate action, enabling batched scoring of all candidates in a single pass.
  • Lightweight scoring heads evaluate candidate latents against the instruction without keeping a large language model or VLM in the loop.
  • Uses a JEPA encoder pretrained on unlabelled GUI screenshots, with evidence that the scorer relies on this latent pathway and that the predictor can be randomly initialized without losing decision performance.

Methodology

GUI-JEPA uses a JEPA encoder pretrained on unlabelled GUI screenshots to produce screen latents. An action- and region-conditioned latent predictor converts the screen latent into one latent per candidate action, and lightweight heads score these candidate latents against the instruction in a single batched pass. It is evaluated on AndroidControl against a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B agent using the same inputs, comparing speed, in-distribution accuracy, and parameter count.

Key Results

On AndroidControl, GUI-JEPA is 2.8-4.0x faster on the same GPU than a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B agent, while reaching 86-92% of its in-distribution accuracy with 6% of its parameters. A randomly initialized predictor performs as well as one pretrained on screen dynamics.

Limitations

  • Accuracy reaches only 86-92% of the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline rather than matching or exceeding it.
  • Evaluation is reported only on AndroidControl, so out-of-distribution or broader GUI generalization is not addressed.
  • Screen-dynamics pretraining of the predictor does not improve decision performance over random initialization, suggesting that this pretraining may be unnecessary for the task.
